# Roamly Deep Interview Transcript Summary

## Outcome

Roamly will launch as an AI itinerary planner for couples/groups. It creates pace-aware multi-day travel plans, enables asynchronous partner feedback, and supports finalized offline trip briefs.

## Decisions reached

1. V1 user: couples/groups planning weekend getaways and shared vacations.
2. Promise: realistic, geographically clustered itineraries generated in seconds.
3. Core: structured form → AI itinerary → owner editing → async partner review → confirmation/offline brief.
4. Collaboration: signed-in invited users vote/comment/suggest; public links are anonymous and view-only.
5. Stack: Expo/React Native, Expo Router native tabs, NativeWind, Node/TypeScript, PostgreSQL/Prisma, Supabase Auth, Mapbox-first maps, PostHog, Sentry.
6. Data policy: use persistable open POI data and present venue details as best effort; direct users to official sites/booking links for confirmation.
7. Privacy: only on-demand foreground location in active-trip mode; privacy-filtered share links; immediate personal-data deletion and group-owner transfer.
8. Scope: no booking, live inventory, public social, synchronous editing, expenses, journals, full offline maps, or full web app.
9. Navigation: Trips, Plan, Profile; discovery is contextual.
10. Pilot/public dates: 23 November 2026 / 21 December 2026, with calendar-duration mismatch recorded as a risk.

## Evidence and research notes

- Google Places policy disallows the initially proposed Google Places + Mapbox usage pattern; its content must not be displayed with a non-Google map and is subject to storage limits.
- Mapbox Search POI results are not permanently storable; Mapbox Places is public preview. Foursquare standard terms also restrict server-side retention.
- Overture/Open Places supports persisted POI data but cannot establish live availability as a product guarantee.
